The money leaks hiding inside everyday work.

The full list

Ten costly gaps. Ten practical fixes.

Each problem begins with the business loss, then works backward to the smallest useful system that can stop it.

01

Leads dying in WhatsApp and Instagram DMs

Top priority

Where the money leaks

High-intent enquiries wait in personal inboxes with no owner or next action. By the time someone replies, the buyer has often moved on.

What I build

A shared lead capture and follow-up tracker with source, status, owner, next action, and reminders.

Useful for: interior designers, solar installers, event planners, and other high-consideration services.

02

Slow quotations

Top priority

Where the money leaks

Manual calculations, repeated questions, and retyping delay the quote. A ready buyer goes to the competitor who replied first.

What I build

A structured intake form that turns customer requirements into a reviewable draft quote.

Useful for: fabricators, installers, agencies, and service businesses with repeatable pricing rules.

03

No-shows and unpaid deposits

Top priority

Where the money leaks

A reserved slot sits empty when the customer does not arrive. Once the day begins, that capacity is difficult or impossible to resell.

What I build

A booking flow with a deposit or payment link, automatic confirmation, and reminders before the appointment.

Useful for: clinics, salons, turf and court bookings, photographers, and appointment-based businesses.

04

Recurring fees chased by hand

Where the money leaks

Staff spend hours checking spreadsheets, sending reminders, and having awkward conversations while cash arrives later than it should.

What I build

A fee ledger with due dates, payment status, automatic reminders, and a clear follow-up queue.

Useful for: tuition centres, coaching institutes, gyms, hostels, and membership businesses.

05

Paper attendance and progress parents never see

Where the money leaks

Records exist, but they do not build parent trust or demonstrate value. Staff must answer the same progress questions again and again.

What I build

Digital attendance, parent notifications, and simple progress reports that make the service visible.

Useful for: academies, tuition centres, coaching institutes, and after-school programmes.

06

“Where is my order?” calls

Where the money leaks

Repeated status calls interrupt the people doing the work, consume staff time, and make an otherwise good service feel uncertain.

What I build

A simple status page customers can check through a link, updated by the team or the existing workflow.

Useful for: tailors, print shops, fabricators, repair centres, and custom manufacturers.

07

Repeat revenue never asked for

Top priority

Where the money leaks

Renewals and recalls depend on someone remembering a future date. When nobody does, valuable repeat business disappears silently.

What I build

A renewal and recall system with due dates, customer history, reminders, and a daily follow-up queue.

Useful for: AMC renewals, vehicle service, dental recalls, insurance renewals, and maintenance businesses.

08

Field jobs tracked through WhatsApp voice notes

Where the money leaks

There is no durable job history, proof of work is scattered, and every repeat visit begins without the information from the last one.

What I build

A technician job log with notes, photos, work completed, parts used, and customer history.

Useful for: AC repair, pest control, appliance service, installations, and mobile service teams.

09

Data retyped between Excel, Tally, and WhatsApp

Where the money leaks

The same order or invoice is entered two or three times. Staff time disappears, mistakes multiply, and nobody trusts the latest version.

What I build

One structured entry point that feeds a clean sheet, invoice, or integration without repeated typing.

Useful for: order desks, distributors, local manufacturers, and businesses running on disconnected tools.

10

Website enquiries nobody reads

Top priority

Where the money leaks

Marketing sends people to a form that fails silently or delivers to an unwatched inbox. The business pays for attention, then loses the lead.

What I build

An audit and repair of the existing lead path, with reliable routing, notifications, and a way to verify that enquiries arrive.

Useful for: any business with an existing website, enquiry form, or paid traffic campaign.

A focused approach

Fix the leak before expanding the system.

The goal is not to add software everywhere. It is to remove the point where a lead, payment, booking, or handoff currently falls through.

01

Find the leak

Trace the actual customer or staff journey and identify where value is lost.

02

Build the shortest useful flow

Design only what is needed to capture, move, and record the work reliably.

03

Connect and verify

Join the necessary tools, test the full path, and make the outcome visible.

Which leak is costing you now?

Describe what gets missed, delayed, repeated by hand, or followed up too late. You do not need to know what the software should be yet.
